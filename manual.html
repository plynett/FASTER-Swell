Technical Information

Users Manual and Technical Basis

FASTER-Swell is a low-computation coastal forecast workflow for estimating maximum wave crest elevation, 2% exceedance runup, overtopping likelihood, and wave-driven flooding potential along California shorelines. The Explorer combines nearshore wave forecasts from the CDIP MOP alongshore model, NOAA tide predictions, and a simplified user-edited beach or structure profile to produce a transect-by-transect operational view.

Primary objective Provide a short-term swell impact forecast that helps interpret flooding, overtopping, berm performance, and pier or wharf exposure.
Hydrodynamic inputs CDIP MOP wave height, peak period, prediction depth, nearest NOAA tide predictions and recent observed water levels in NAVD88, plus wind-speed data for local chop.
Geometry inputs User-defined slope, toe elevation, toe distance, and crest geometry, plus an auto-estimated sea-level anomaly that remains editable.
Outputs Wave, tide, wind, and chop playback; maximum crest; expected runup; conservative runup; upper-bound runup; and overtopping category.

Purpose and Scope

The background documents describe FASTER-Swell as a forecast tool for large wind-wave and swell events, not a tsunami model. The method is intended for event windows on the order of several days and is designed to answer questions such as: How high can wave crests reach near a pier deck? Will a temporary berm or dune likely be overtopped? Is wave-driven flooding likely on a coastal road or backshore area?

The operational concept is to start with wave conditions already predicted by the MOP system near the 10 m depth contour, combine those conditions with NOAA tide predictions, a recent observed-minus-predicted sea-level anomaly, a wind-derived local chop proxy, and a user-defined shoreline geometry, then evaluate several empirical runup formulations to produce decision-ready water-level estimates.

Operational Data Flow

  1. The Explorer loads statewide MOP transects from the compact data/transects.json web export.
  2. When a transect is selected, the app requests the corresponding CDIP MOP forecast directly from the THREDDS DAP2 ASCII endpoint. If the URL includes start_date=MMDDYYYY, the app instead requests an up-to-five-day CDIP MOP historical window from the matching nowcast or hindcast file. This URL mode is hindcast-only; if the requested five-day window would extend into the present, the end time is clipped to a safe historical hour before now so upstream nowcast files have time to land.
  3. The app resolves the nearest NOAA tide station from the California station list, downloads hourly tide predictions in NAVD88, and estimates observed-minus-predicted sea-level anomaly from NOAA water levels. Forecast mode uses the recent residual window; hindcast mode uses the selected historical window.
  4. In forecast mode, the app requests NWS gridpoint wind-speed forecasts at the transect prediction point and converts those speeds into a local chop-height time series. In hindcast mode, the app requests hourly 10 m historical/reanalysis wind speed from Open-Meteo at the transect prediction point and runs the same chop calculation.
  5. The Geometry Editor defines a stylized profile using the user inputs. Dune mode returns landward to the toe elevation; structure mode remains elevated landward of crest.
  6. For each forecast time, the app computes equivalent deep-water wave properties, breaker conditions, runup estimates, and summary water levels.
  7. The Explorer displays the wave, tide, wind, and chop time series, allows playback through forecast time, and plots the selected-time water levels across the edited profile.

Variable Definitions and Data Sources

Units and datums

The browser model computes internally in metric units. Elevations and water levels are meters relative to NAVD88 unless noted otherwise, distances are meters, wave periods are seconds, wind speed is converted to meters per second for the chop calculation, and angles are radians inside the equations unless a degree-valued source field is explicitly noted.

External forecast and observation variables

Live MOP variables come from the CDIP THREDDS DAP2 ASCII endpoint. If that request is unavailable and a bundled fallback exists, the same fields are read from data/forecasts.

Prediction-point water depth, from CDIP MOP metaWaterDepth.
Dp

User-edited geometry and anomaly variables

These variables are editable in the Geometry Editor. Initial geometry priority is surveyed values in data/known_transects.csv, then a valid DEMTransects/<transect>.txt profile, then baseline defaults from data/forecast-manifest.json or generic settings with a MOP-based slope estimate. DEM text files contain five numeric geometry parameters in meters NAVD88 and a dimensionless slope; the Explorer converts the display to the selected units.

Set to Default restores baseline settings without surveyed or DEM overrides. Set to DEM-Based Transect and Set to Surveyed Transect appear only when those data are available. Selecting either applies its five geometry values and immediately updates the profile and available runup results, retaining the current sea-level anomaly and Dune/Structure mode. The highlighted button identifies the selected source; manual edits clear that highlight. Inspect DEM terrain opens available comparison and diagnostic images in a pop-out, including locations where a five-parameter fit could not be determined. Use the survey selector to compare attempts, or the review link for details; use Close or Escape to dismiss it.

Terrain inspection does not apply a profile or enable the DEM button. Candidate fits awaiting review remain separate from published geometry. Where measured terrain exists but fitting failed, the image can guide manual geometry edits; where no measured pixels exist, the image reports the source or processing failure. Survey years are shown because older terrain may no longer represent current beach conditions. The static DEMTransects/images.json index references retained images, independently of the optional five-parameter text files. Geometry text files remain in DEMTransects; comparison JPEGs are retrieved from https://celeris.usc.edu/FASTER-Swell/DEMImages/. The main comparison is named <transect>.jpg, and alternate survey images use source-specific suffixes.

The OC pilot and expansion now provide 320 experimental DEM profiles for testing. Another 100 fitted candidates exceed the existing Explorer geometry limits and remain inspection-only; values are not clipped to make them load. The remaining unfitted locations retain their diagnostic images. The map uses the original teal for default geometry, purple for an available DEM profile, and magenta for an available surveyed profile. Surveyed availability takes precedence. Colors describe available data, not the currently applied or manually edited geometry; selection and hover use thicker lines.

The OC399 DEM profile is an experimental 2023-lidar baseline, not a surveyed replacement. Its toe lies on measured terrain and is constrained to 2-3 m NAVD88; its slope connects the last measured 1 m crossing before the toe to that toe. The measured elevated-berm midpoint provides a crest reference; the Gaussian peak position is then fitted to the ocean-facing slope while its elevation is fixed. The landward terrain does not contribute to the fitting error. Inspect the matching comparison image and survey year before using any DEM alternative.

Geometry Representation

Beach face and toe location

zmin = -5 m (internal reference)
xlinear,end = (ztoe - zmin) / β
xflat = xlinear,end + Wtoe

The Explorer builds the seaward profile from a constant average slope β up to the dune or structure toe elevation, then applies a flat toe/berm segment.

Source: current Explorer implementation in app.js.

Dune or structure crest shape

Wd = 2 Wtoe-crest
xc = xflat + Wd / 2
A = zcrest - ztoe
σ = Wd / 6
z(x) = ztoe + A exp(-(x - xc)2 / (2σ2))

The dune/structure crest is represented with a Gaussian rise over the specified toe-to-crest distance. In dune mode the landward side returns to the toe elevation; in structure mode the landward side remains at crest elevation.

Source: current Explorer implementation in app.js.

Hydrodynamic and Runup Equations

Terminology note

The local project files sometimes refer to the Stockdon formulation as "Stockton." In this manual the published name Stockdon et al. (2006) is used. The formulas below match the project formula sheet and the current code path.

1. Water-level anomaly and local-wind preprocessing

Sea-level anomaly from NOAA residuals

ASL = mean(ηobs - ηpred)
η(t) = ηNOAA,pred(t) + ASL

The Explorer uses the nearest NOAA tide station, NAVD88 datum, and a three-day lookback ending one hour before the run. Observed water levels are paired to six-minute predictions in the same datum, with a minimum of 24 paired residuals. If the residual estimate is unavailable, the current sea-level anomaly field value is retained.

Source: current Explorer implementation in app.js.

Local chop proxy from wind speed

Hchop(U10; Tc) = 0.241 (U102 / g) √{1 - exp[-1.25 ((0.13 g Tc) / U10)4]}
Tc = 4 s
Htotal = Hswell + Hchop

The Explorer converts the active wind-speed source to U10 in m/s, evaluates this short-period Pierson-Moskowitz tail proxy at each playback time, and plots the resulting Hchop series on the wind panel. Forecast mode uses NWS gridpoint forecast winds. Hindcast mode uses Open-Meteo hourly historical/reanalysis winds. Non-positive or unavailable wind speeds return Hchop = 0 for that time.

Source: Chop Height Proxy Model.pdf; current implementation in app.js.

2. Conversion from nearshore prediction point to equivalent deep-water properties

Shallow-water and deep-water wavelengths

Ls = (g Tp2 / 2π) √tanh(4π2d / (Tp2 g))
Lo = g Tp2 / (2π)

These are the nearshore and equivalent deep-water wavelengths used throughout the transformation and runup calculations.

Source: project formulas sheet; implemented in FASTER_MOPS.py and app.js.

Group factor, shoaling, and operational deep-water wave height

k = 2π / Ls
ns = 0.5 [1 + 2kd / sinh(2kd)]
angs = 0, angd = 0 (operational baseline)
Ks = √[nsLs / (0.5Lo)]
Kr = 1
Ho = Ks Kr Hs,swell

The documented directional method uses wave direction relative to shoreline normal, but the current Python lineage and Explorer code set the operational directional term to zero inside the timestep loop. MOP wave direction and shore-normal metadata are loaded, but the live runup calculation behaves as this non-directional baseline.

Sources: background docx, formulas sheet, O'Reilly et al. (2016), current code.

3. Breaking-wave calculation and maximum crest elevation

CEM-style breaking relation

a = 43.75 [1 - exp(-19β)]
b = 1.56 / [1 + exp(-19.5β)]
Hb = Ho Ks,b Kr,b
dbreaking = Hb / [b - a Hb / Tp2]

The solver iterates on breaking depth until the Coastal Engineering Manual style breaking relation is satisfied. If no stable bracket is found, the code falls back to Hb = Hswell and dbreaking = Hswell.

Source: project formulas sheet, attributed there to the Coastal Engineering Manual breaking-wave-height method; implemented in Python and web code.

Maximum-considered wave crest

zcrest,max = η + Hb MaxRatio CrestRatio
zsplash,max = η + 2Hb MaxRatio CrestRatio
MaxRatio = 1.3
CrestRatio = 0.55

Here η is NOAA tide prediction plus the current sea-level anomaly. The formula sheet treats 1.3 as a representative ratio between maximum-considered wave height and significant wave height, and 0.55 as the FEMA crest fraction of total wave height. Where a matching pier transect file exists, the runup profile also plots zsplash,max as a thin gray dashed reference line.

Source: project formulas sheet; implemented in FASTER_MOPS.py and app.js.

4. 2% exceedance runup formulas

Stockdon et al. (2006) total runup

SetupStockdon = 1.1 0.35 β √(LoHo)
SwashStockdon = 1.1 0.5 √[HoLo(0.563β2 + 0.0004)]
R2,Stockdon = η + SetupStockdon + SwashStockdon

The project formulas sheet also shows an angle-reduction factor, but the current operational code path does not actively apply it.

Source: Stockdon et al. (2006); project formulas sheet; current implementation.

Park and Cox (2016) berm and dune adjustment

Cμ = 0.8 - 0.4 tanh(2Wb / Lo)
Cσ = 1.0 - 0.5 tanh(2Wb / Lo)
βf = arctan(β)
βd = arctan[(zcrest - ztoe) / (0.5Wd)]
H* = max(Ho, 0.05 m), L* = max(Lo, 0.05 m)
α = min(1, max(0, ((η - ztoe) + 0.7H*) / (1.4H*)))
βt = (1 - α)βf + αβd
βuse = βf if (η - ztoe) / H* < -0.7; βd if > 0.7; otherwise βt
ξ = tan(βuse) / √(H*/L*)
R2,Park,μ = η + 1.35 Cμ H* ξ0.65
R2,Park,σ = η + 1.35 Cσ H* ξ0.65

The code uses a piecewise choice for βuse: foreshore angle for low water levels, dune/structure face angle for high water levels, and transition angle in between.

Source: Park and Cox (2016); project formulas sheet; current implementation.

Modified Stockdon with Park-style geometry terms

Setupmod,μ = 1.1 Cμ 0.35 tan(βuse) √(L*H*)
Swashmod,μ = 1.1 0.5 Cμ √[H*L*(0.563 tanh2(βuse) + 0.0004)]
R2,mod,μ = η + Setupmod,μ + Swashmod,μ
R2,mod,σ uses Cσ in the same two terms

This hybrid preserves the Stockdon structure while introducing the berm and dune/structure geometry sensitivity from Park and Cox.

Source: project formulas sheet; current implementation.

Oblique-wave reduction factor

γθ = cos(angd)0.4 + 2.7 tan(β)

This factor appears in the formulas sheet and is attributed there to Ormondt et al. (2021). It is useful conceptually, but it is currently inactive in the main Python and browser timestep loop because the live operational runup calculation is using the non-directional baseline noted above.

Source: project formulas sheet; Ormondt et al. (2021) as cited there.

5. Summary metrics used in the Explorer

Expected, conservative, and upper-bound runup

Rmean = mean(R2,Stockdon, R2,Park,μ, R2,mod,μ)
sR = stdev(R2,Stockdon, R2,Park,μ, R2,Park,σ, R2,mod,μ, R2,mod,σ)
FLP = (Tp / 18 s)4
ΔH = (Htotal - Hswell) / 2 = Hchop / 2
Expected = Rmean
Conservative = Rmean + FLPsR
Upper Bound = Conservative + ΔH

The long period swell uncertainty factor increases to the fourth power with peak period relative to an 18 s reference period. It is applied to the conservative runup spread to account for potential model error in long-period swell. The upper-bound term then adds the difference between total wave height and swell wave height as a simple allowance for local wind-wave or chop uncertainty. In the Explorer, this local chop height is estimated from the active wind-speed source at each playback time.

Source: project formulas sheet; current implementation.

Overtopping category logic

If Expected > zcrest : Significant overtopping probable
Else if Conservative > zcrest : Moderate overtopping possible
Else if Upper Bound > zcrest or Spectral-Weighted elevation > zcrest : Minor overtopping possible
Else if flat berm width > 10 ft (3.048 m) and any finite runup elevation > toe elevation : Possible Lower Beach Overwash
Else : Overtopping not expected

This is the decision logic that drives the category pill in the Runup Output panel. Minor overtopping is possible when either the Upper Bound or Spectral-Weighted elevation exceeds the crest, but Conservative does not. Significant and Moderate categories take precedence. An elevation equal to the crest does not trigger exceedance; unavailable spectral elevations are ignored.

Possible Lower Beach Overwash applies only at times with no crest overtopping, where Distance from Swash to Dune/Structure Toe is strictly greater than 10 feet (3.048 meters), and Expected, Conservative, Upper Bound, or Spectral-Weighted runup elevation is strictly above the toe elevation. These elevations already include tide and anomaly; neither is added again. Equality at either threshold does not trigger the warning. Maximum crest and splash-up are not runup metrics for this check. The warning appears in the selected-time figure title and in the forecast summary if no higher overtopping category occurs. It remains a no-crest-overtopping condition and does not add tide-panel shading.

Source: current Explorer implementation in app.js.

How to Use the Explorer

  1. Zoom the Selection Map until local transects appear, then click the transect of interest.
  2. Use the top-row toggles in the Geometry Editor to choose dune vs structure geometry, Metric vs English display units, and UTC vs Local Time display. The Explorer opens in English units and Local Time by default.
  3. Review the auto-estimated average beach-face slope and sea-level anomaly, and edit the profile elevations and distances as needed.
  4. Select a transect from the map to automatically download live MOP forecast data and the nearest NOAA tide prediction, then populate the lower playback panels. To run a historical window of up to five days, add start_date=MMDDYYYY to the URL, for example ?transect=OC400&start_date=08212022. Recent start dates are clipped before the current time rather than blended with forecast data.
  5. Edit the beach or structure geometry inputs and the runup and overtopping analysis will refresh automatically.
  6. Use the slider or Play button to move through forecast time and inspect how waves, tides, and runup vary through the event window.
  7. The tide curve stays green throughout. Translucent background bands indicate overtopping: yellow for Minor and red for Moderate or Significant, with no shading when overtopping is not indicated. Beach bands start 30 minutes before and after each model time. Each side independently extends to 90 minutes if the interpolated tide at 30 minutes on that side exceeds the tide at the model time. Bands span the full vertical plot height behind the curve, and overlapping windows use the more severe color. Bands are clipped to the displayed time range; these are visual windows around model samples, not independently predicted event durations. Shading updates automatically with geometry changes. A beach tide-only preview without wave data has no overtopping assessment or shading; Bay Points use the independent calculation described below.

Discrete Amplitude Spectrum

The Wave/Tide panel includes a period spectrum synchronized with the selected forecast time. CDIP frequency-band variance is redistributed into one-second period bins centered on integer periods. For example, the 12-second bin covers 11.5 to 12.5 seconds. Energy density is treated as constant within each source frequency band.

fj,min = 1 / (Tj + 0.5 s),   fj,max = 1 / (Tj - 0.5 s)
Vj = ∑i Ei Δfi × overlap([fi,min, fi,max], [fj,min, fj,max]) / (fi,max - fi,min)
aj = √(2 Vj),   Hs,reconstructed = 4 √(∑j Vj)

Here E is CDIP variance spectral density in m²/Hz, Δf is the supplied bandwidth in Hz, and overlap is the overlapping frequency width. Each bar is an equivalent sinusoidal component amplitude in meters or feet. These amplitudes are not significant wave heights; their variances add. Lighter edge bins are only partially covered by the source spectrum. The vertical scale stays fixed for the loaded forecast, allowing amplitudes to be compared during playback.

The spectrum display shows the 5 to 25 second bins; the spectral-weighted runup calculation uses all available bins, including partially covered edge bins. Spectra failing CDIP quality checks or containing missing energy values are marked unavailable. The bulk-wave runup calculation remains unchanged.

Source spectral definitions: CDIP MOP output specification. Conversion: variance-preserving allocation to period bins with a²/2 equal to bin variance.

Spectral-Weighted Runup (Experimental)

For each one-second period bin, the Explorer evaluates the Expected runup approach using the bin center period Tj and wave height Hj = 2aj. It uses the mean of the Stockdon, Park mean-berm, and modified Stockdon mean-berm predictions, with the same prediction depth, beach geometry, still-water elevation, and inverse shoaling as the bulk-wave calculation. No standard deviation or long-period uncertainty multiplier is added. Period still enters the underlying wavelength and runup equations.

Hj = 2aj
rj = mean(rStockdon,j, rPark,μ,j, rmodified,μ,j)
rspectral = √(∑j rj2)
zplotted = NOAA tide + sea-level anomaly + rspectral

The wave-only equations return a runup height for each bin directly; no tide or anomaly is added to or subtracted from those outputs. The spectral helper combines those heights into rspectral. The Explorer then adds tide and anomaly once to obtain the plotted elevation. Actual water level still affects the model's beach/dune geometry response; this is separate from the elevation offset. Zero-energy bins contribute zero. Positive-height bins retain the existing model's 0.05 m inverse-shoaled-height safeguard. Wind-derived chop is not included in this spectral combination because it is not part of the CDIP amplitude spectrum.

The magenta dotted line updates with the selected time and geometry. It is omitted when valid spectra are unavailable. This is an experimental combination, not a separately validated R2% forecast method. It does not replace the existing expected, conservative, or upper-bound calculations. A spectral elevation exceeding the crest can trigger Minor overtopping possible when the Conservative elevation does not exceed the crest; Significant and Moderate categories still take precedence.

Basis: the existing Stockdon, Park and Cox, and modified Stockdon Expected ensemble described above; the spectral root-sum-square combination is a project-specific experimental extension.

How to Read the Output

  • MOP Wave Forecast: blue line is significant wave height Hs, red dashed line is peak period Tp.
  • NOAA Tide Prediction: tide level in NAVD88, with the current sea-level anomaly incorporated into the runup metrics.
  • Discrete Amplitude Spectrum: CDIP swell component amplitudes in one-second period bins at the selected slider time, with the largest bin highlighted in teal. This spectrum excludes the separately calculated wind-derived chop.
  • NWS Wind Forecast: gold line is wind speed and navy dashed line is the local chop-height proxy estimated from that wind speed.
  • Playback metrics: Wave Hs, Local Chop, Peak Period, Tide + Anomaly, Max Crest, Expected 2%, Conservative, and Upper Bound all correspond to the selected slider time.
  • Runup profile: shows still water, maximum crest, expected runup, conservative runup, and upper-bound runup projected across the edited profile. Where a matching pier file exists, a thick dark-brown line shows the pier deck elevation profile and a thin gray dashed line shows maximum wave splash-up.
  • Spectral-Weighted Runup: magenta dotted line is the experimental root-sum-square combination of per-period conservative runup rises, plus still water once.
  • Category pill: summarizes the highest overtopping category in the displayed forecast window, including spectral-triggered Minor overtopping. If there is no crest overtopping, it reports Possible Lower Beach Overwash when that condition occurs. The figure title reports the condition at the selected time.

Current Implementation Notes

  • The Explorer is fully browser-side at runtime. Live MOP forecasts and historical MOP nowcast/hindcast windows are pulled directly from CDIP THREDDS, NOAA tides and water levels are pulled directly from NOAA CO-OPS, current wind forecasts are pulled directly from the NWS API, and hindcast wind speeds are pulled from the Open-Meteo historical weather API.
  • The nearest NOAA tide station is resolved from the California tide-station list included with the web app.
  • The average beach-face slope is auto-estimated from MOP transect geometry as two times the MOP average slope: 2 x prediction depth divided by backbeach-to-prediction-point distance. The value is then limited to the model slope range. This is a swash-zone proxy, not a replacement for surveyed foreshore profile data.
  • The current live Python and browser code path uses the non-directional baseline during the runup calculation loop, even though the formulas sheet includes directional wave-angle terms and an oblique-wave reduction factor.
  • The sea-level anomaly is auto-estimated from NOAA observed-minus-predicted water levels. Forecast mode uses the recent residual window; hindcast mode uses the selected historical playback window.
  • Local chop is auto-estimated from wind speed using the four-second Pierson-Moskowitz short-period proxy documented in the project notes. If no wind interval covers a playback time, the displayed and modeled local chop value for that time is zero.
  • The profile is stylized and parameterized. It is not a direct survey profile unless those parameters have been tuned to represent local conditions.
  • If no live MOP forecast is available for a transect, the Explorer falls back to a tide-only preview mode.

Bay Points: Huntington Harbor

The Bay Point marker shrinks when zooming out and hides at the same zoom threshold as the beach transects. Bay profiles use the same plot styling as beach profiles. The geometry preview shows a still-water reference at 0 NAVD88. Flooding plot vertical limits cover the full forecast and remain fixed during playback; they are recalculated only when forecast data or the ground elevation changes. Horizontal position is schematic, not surveyed distance.

Open Huntington Harbor at 33.721485, -118.064714. Bay Points are point-level flooding estimates, not beach transects. The default ground/structure elevation here is 6.5 ft (1.9812 m) NAVD88, a user-specified starting value rather than a surveyed elevation. Edit this geometry input to represent the local ground or wall. The Water Level Fluctuation / Uncertainty input, beside Sea-Level Anomaly, supplies a nonnegative allowance Δ, defaulting to zero at this location. It is not part of the measured anomaly or a calibrated confidence interval. Changes immediately update the flooding assessment. Set to Default restores both site-specific defaults from data/bay-points.json. Metric/English units, UTC/local time, and playback operate as for beaches.

The initial water-level source is NOAA 9410660, Los Angeles (Outer Harbor), an outside-harbor proxy with NAVD88 predictions and observations. Nearby Los Patos bridge (TWC0427) is a subordinate prediction site without a verified NAVD88 observation series; Long Beach Fire Boat Pier (9410678) does not currently provide NAVD88 through the NOAA API. No harbor lag or attenuation correction is applied. The source is shown in the Geometry Editor.

A = mean(observed water level - predicted tide) over the anomaly estimation window
Zwater(t) = T(t) + A
Zuncertainty(t) = T(t) + A + Δ
Zchop(t) = T(t) + A + Δ + C(t)

T(t) is the six-minute NOAA tide prediction in NAVD88. In forecast mode, A is the latest three-day mean residual, using the same one-hour observation safety lag and pairing rules as the beach calculation, held constant over seven days. In hindcast mode, A is the mean residual over the selected historical window, matching the beach hindcast approach. This historical-window average uses observations throughout that window; it is a retrospective assessment, not a reconstruction of what could have been forecast at its start.

Fetch-Limited Harbor Chop

Huntington Harbor uses F = 1500 m in every wind direction, stored as worstCaseFetchMeters in data/bay-points.json. Bay chop uses the fetch-dependent JONSWAP formulation below; beach transects retain their existing Pierson-Moskowitz proxy. U is the 10-m wind speed in m/s, g = 9.81 m/s2, and f is frequency in Hz.

X = gF/U2
α = 0.076 X-0.22
fp = 3.5 (g/U) X-0.33
S(f) = [αg2 / ((2π)4f5)] exp[-1.25(fp/f)4] γr
r = exp[-(f/fp - 1)2 / (2σ2)]
γ = 3.3; σ = 0.07 for f ≤ fp, otherwise 0.09
C(t) = 4 [∫0.25∞ S(f) df]1/2

References: Hasselmann et al. (1973), as summarized in BOEM 2018-057, equation 5-13 and table 5-3; spectral significant wave height definition. Numerical integration covers all frequencies above 0.25 Hz (periods below four seconds). Calm wind gives zero chop; missing or invalid wind/fetch gives unavailable chop.

This is an experimental sustained-wind, deep-water estimate using a fixed worst-case fetch, not a guaranteed upper bound. No depth, wind-duration, sheltering, or reflection correction is applied, and the estimate has not been calibrated for this harbor. C is a significant wave height, not a statistically defined maximum crest; adding its full value to water level remains the existing screening allowance.

  • Major flooding expected: T + A exceeds ground/structure elevation by more than 0.5 ft (0.1524 m), regardless of uncertainty or chop availability. Exactly 0.5 ft above ground is Moderate, not Major.
  • Moderate flooding expected: Major is not met, and either T + A or T + A + Δ exceeds ground. This includes still-water exceedances up to and including 0.5 ft above ground.
  • Minor flooding expected: T + A + Δ + C exceeds ground, but T + A + Δ does not.
  • Flooding not expected: all three assessed levels remain at or below ground. Equality is not exceedance. Major takes precedence over Moderate, which takes precedence over Minor.
  • Unavailable data: a missing anomaly prevents a flooding assessment. Missing chop leaves the chop-based assessment unavailable, rather than assigning zero; known Major and Moderate exceedances are still reported. The summary identifies unassessed times.
  • Entrance conditions: OC641 waves and spectra are displayed for context only. They never enter the bay flooding calculation, and their absence does not prevent bay results. Spectra show the nearest available native MOP forecast time, explicitly labeled.
  • Flooding Output: the schematic wall shows ground and all three water-level estimates, labeled in the legend. Major classification compares the base water + anomaly level with ground plus 0.5 ft; the plotted levels themselves are unchanged. Yellow tide shading marks Minor; red marks Moderate or Major. Shading follows six-minute assessments, not the beach-specific sample-band extension rule. The header summarizes the maximum available forecast classification; the figure title describes the selected time. These thresholds apply to Bay Points in both forecast and hindcast modes; beach runup thresholds are unchanged.

This experimental estimate does not simulate inundation extent, tidal propagation, harbor circulation, rainfall/drainage, boat wakes, or changing storm surge. It should be checked against local observations and surveyed elevations before operational decisions.
